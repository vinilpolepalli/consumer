Where the numbers come from

Lifetime tab: one round × how often × every year until you turn 65. Today’s price, honestly multiplied — no inflation games, no price creep.

Invested instead: the same money, put into a broad index fund each year, growing about 7% a year — the long-run U.S. market average after inflation. Real returns bounce around. Seven percent is the boring middle.

Why 65? The classic retirement age. If you’re already past it, the receipt runs your next ten years instead.

Both numbers are estimates, and neither is financial advice. The point isn’t precision. The point is the gap between them.
