# Last Visits — three ready-to-film reels

All three are filmable by one person with one phone in under an hour, total.
Shared prep (10 min, do once):

- Charge phone, Do Not Disturb ON, brightness ~80%, dark room with one warm lamp.
- Open the live app URL, run it once so inputs are warm, then Settings → Erase
  everything so the reveal and the one free planner slide are fresh for camera.
- Screen recordings: iOS Control Center screen recorder, microphone OFF. The reveal
  was built silence-proof — do not add music to the reveal beat. Ever.
- Face shots: prop the phone against books, front camera, the app itself lights your
  face. No ring light — the glow from the screen is the shot.

Rule for all captions: the app link is in bio and pinned comment, never watermarked
on the video. Numbers in captions always say "about."

---

## Reel 1 — "14" (format: The gut-punch stat reveal)

**This is the flagship. Film and post this one first.**

**Hook line (0.0–1.5s, white text on black, nothing else on screen):**

> You will see your mom about 14 more times.

**Shot-by-shot:**

| Time | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5s | Black frame, hook line in white serif. No logo, no face. | Muffled heartbeat or full silence. Text appears instantly — no fade-in. |
| 1.5–4s | Screen recording: thumb taps "Mom", steps age to 61, taps "Twice a year", taps **Show me the number**. | Real speed, no cuts. The "Free. No account." footer stays visible — leave it in frame, it pre-answers the top comment. |
| 4–8s | The reveal: amber dots flood, the flood *stops*, white dots ignite one by one and pulse, counter rolls and freezes. | Do not trim the still hold after the freeze. The 2 seconds of nothing is the reel. |
| 8–11s | Cut to your face lit only by the phone. Say nothing. On-screen text, small: "I call her every Sunday now." | One take, eyes on screen not camera. If it feels embarrassing, it's working. |
| 11–15s | Back to screen recording: planner slider drags "twice a year" → "monthly". Gray dots re-ignite amber, "+N visits" floats up. Final text: "The number isn't fixed." | End frozen on the re-lit grid. No outro card. |

**Caption:**

> I typed two things and I haven't been the same since. It's free, it's in my bio,
> and your number is different from mine. Tell me what you got. About, not exactly —
> the app rounds up in her favor on purpose.

**Why this format fits:** This app *is* the gut-punch stat productized — Jesse
Itzler's talking-head version of this exact stat pulled ~4.9M likes with no app to
download. Every viewer's number is different, which turns the comments into a wall of
"I got 22" replies, and each reply is a new person who opened the app.

## Reel 2 — "340 vs 12" (format: Tag-a-friend result)

**Hook line (0.0–1.5s, two share cards side by side, text on top):**

> Her number is 340. Mine is 12.

**Shot-by-shot:**

| Time | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prep | Run the app twice: once as someone who lives near their mom ("Weekly+", young parent), once as you ("Once a year"). Save both Ember share cards (9:16). | 5 minutes. Two runs, two cards, done — no second person needed on camera. |
| 0–1.5s | Both cards side by side, hook text over them. | The two dot grids do the talking: one dense wall of white, one almost empty. |
| 1.5–5s | Zoom slowly into YOUR card. Text: "I moved abroad. I fly home once a year." | This is the diaspora angle — the most underserved, highest-pain audience for this app. |
| 5–9s | Screen recording: change one chip, "Once a year" → "A few times a year". Dots re-ignite, "+N visits". | The fix, on camera, in real time. |
| 9–13s | Your face, phone-lit. Text: "Send this to the friend who moved away. Or the one who should call their mom." | Straight to camera this time. |
| 13–15s | End on your card, small text: "What's your number?" | The card's built-in tag toggle exists for exactly this — flip it on. |

**Caption:**

> Tag the friend who moved away. Don't say anything else, just tag them. Free, no
> account, in my bio. (Both numbers say "about" — it's an estimate, rounded up in
> Mom's favor.)

**Why this format fits:** The share card is a two-sided artifact by design — the app
ships a "What's your number?" tag toggle and a 9:16 story size because comparison is
the loop. Tag-a-friend outsources distribution to the comment section, and the
near/far contrast makes immigrant and college-leaver viewers — the target user —
feel personally found.

## Reel 3 — "I built this and I regret checking" (format: Founder build-in-public)

**Hook line (0.0–1.5s, you, phone-lit, straight to camera):**

> I built an app this week and testing it ruined me.

**Shot-by-shot:**

| Time | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.5s | Face to camera, dark room, hook line spoken AND as on-screen text. | Slightly tired is good. You did just ship an app. |
| 1.5–4s | Screen over shoulder or screen recording: "The idea: type your mom's age, how often you fly home. It shows the visits you have left." | Spoken fast, cut fast. This beat is setup, don't linger. |
| 4–8s | Screen recording of YOUR real reveal. Let the counter freeze. Beat of silence. | Your genuine number. If you flinch on camera, keep the take. |
| 8–12s | "So I built the second half: watch." Planner slider, dots re-ignite, "+N visits." | The founder story earns showing the paywall-side feature free of charge. |
| 12–15s | Face again: "It's free while it's early. If your number wrecks you too — the fix is one slider away." | End on face, not logo. |

**Caption:**

> Day 1 of shipping an app nobody asked for and everybody needs. Free in bio while
> it's early access. Comment your number and I'll reply to every single one.

**Why this format fits:** Works before the app has any traction — the story is the
founder, not the install count, and "I made a thing that hurt me" is a proven
build-in-public hook. It also sets up the series: Day 5 ("someone commented they
booked a flight"), Day 12 ("it hit the charts"), each one a new reel with zero new
ideas needed. Post this 2–3 days after Reel 1 so the account has a spine.

---

## Formats deliberately skipped

- **Brutal honesty:** banned by the brand. This app never roasts, never guilts —
  the number does the work. A roast-format reel would poison the product's one rule.
- **POV demo:** the app has no camera/pointing mechanic; the reveal recording
  already is the demo, and Reel 1 uses it better.
