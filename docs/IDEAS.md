## Full idea sheets

### Last Visits — 7.67

Type your mom's age and how often you fly home; the app shows the shockingly small number of times you'll ever see her again — as a wall of dots you can't unsee — then shows you exactly how to raise the number.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s): Black screen, white text, muffled heartbeat audio: "You will see your mom 14 more times." BEAT 2 (1-4s): Screen recording — fingers type "Mom, 61" and tap "I see her about twice a year." BEAT 3 (4-8s): The app renders a grid of dots. Amber dots flood in fast ("visits you've already had"), then the animation stops and only 14 white dots remain, pulsing against a field of gray. A counter rolls down and freezes on 14. Slow zoom, no music. BEAT 4 (8-12s): Cut to creator's face lit by the phone in a dark room, silent, eyes glassy. On-screen text: "I call her every Sunday now." BEAT 5 (12-15s): Back in the app, they change "twice a year" to "once a month" and 9 gray dots re-ignite amber: "+9 visits." Text: "The number isn't fixed." CTA (after 50% watch, on-screen text only): "It's free to see your number. Search LAST VISITS on the App Store." No link-in-bio.
- **Why it spreads:** It productizes the single most proven stat in this genre — Jesse Itzler's 'how many times will you see your parents again' pulled ~4.9M likes as a talking-head video with no app attached. Every viewer's number is different, which makes it a reaction/duet format (face -> input -> reveal -> tears), the same engine as Death Clock's press cycle. Comment section becomes a grief ritual ('calling my mom rn'), which is pure comment-velocity fuel. The dot-grid card is a born screenshot: dark, minimal, with one horrifying number. Diaspora/immigrant TikTok (kids who fly home once a year) makes the math even more brutal and is a huge underserved audience cluster for seeding.
- **Target user:** Adults 22-40 living away from their parents — especially immigrants and college-leavers who see family once or twice a year and carry low-grade guilt about it.
- **Core loop:** Weekly Sunday notification ('You have N visits left. Next one planned?'). Log a visit or a call and a dot re-lights with a satisfying animation; a call streak builds. Home-screen widget shows the remaining dots for Mom, so the number stares at you daily. Planner lets you simulate 'what if I visited every 2 months' and watch dots come back — the emotional reward loop is literally adding time with your parents.
- **Monetization:** Free: one person (Mom), the full reveal, and one share card — the reveal must stay free because it IS the ad. Paywall hits immediately after the first reveal: unlock the whole family (dad, grandparents, siblings), the home-screen widget, the 'change the math' planner, call streaks, and premium card themes. $4.99/week with 3-day trial (utilities meta), anchored against $29.99/year and a $39.99 lifetime for subscription-averse users.
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 4 days
- **Competitors:** seeyourfolks.com (2013 web calculator, zero polish, no app), Death Clock (generalist mortality at $19.99/mo, not family-specific), life-in-weeks apps like Lifev (grids without the visits framing). Nobody owns the 'visits left' artifact plus widget plus planner.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** Everything paid is a repetition of a trick the free reveal just taught the user; the marginal value of person #2 is arithmetic they can now do without you.

### The Tab — 7.33

Type any habit — DoorDash, lattes, vapes — and a slot-machine receipt reveals your lifetime tab, then flips it to the number that hurts: what it'd be worth by 65 if you'd invested it.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s, on-screen text, face-cam, sound-off legible): "I'm 24 and my DoorDash habit is apparently costing me $486,000." BEATS: (1) 1-4s screen-record: creator types '$19 DoorDash order', taps '4x a week', drags age slider to 24. (2) 4-7s: taps the big red 'RUN MY TAB' button — digits roll like a slot machine to LIFETIME TAB: $71,240, then the screen flips with a haptic thud to 'IF YOU'D INVESTED IT: $486,912 BY 65' as a CVS-length receipt unrolls past the bottom of the frame. (3) 7-12s: hard cut back to face-cam — silent, hands on head, no words (this is the duet/stitch bait frame). (4) 12-15s CTA after 50% watch, no link-in-bio: text overlay 'run your own tab — search THE TAB on the app store. comment a habit and I'll run it next.' Comment farm: 'do vaping', 'do my girlfriend's Sephora', 'I am NOT running mine.'
- **Why it spreads:** It's the Death Clock formula ported to money: a shocking personalized number that exists for every single viewer, so watching someone else's tab immediately creates the itch to run your own. The receipt card is a built-in 9:16 shareable artifact (confession content: 'my tab is worse than yours'), the 'comment a habit' loop generates infinite creator follow-ups, and the invested-instead flip triggers frugality-war comment velocity ('it's $5, live your life' vs 'this is why you're broke'). Journalists already write 'I tried the app that says my coffee costs $200K' pieces for free.
- **Target user:** 18-30 TikTok natives with guilt-spend habits (DoorDash, coffee, vapes, Ubers, girl-math purchases) — the audience already fluent in 'girl math' and 'latte factor' discourse.
- **Core loop:** Run a tab (15 seconds: habit, cost, frequency, age) -> slot-machine reveal -> share/save receipt card -> stack more habits into a Full Life Audit -> install the 'Burning Now' home-screen widget that ticks up in real time what today's habits have cost so far -> daily 'skipped it' check-in builds a save-streak that converts burned money into a projected-wealth graph. The widget + streak is the daily retention loop and the App Store 4.2 native differentiator.
- **Monetization:** Free: one habit tab + shareable receipt card (the marketing must stay free). Curiosity-gap paywall immediately after first reveal: 'Your FULL life tab across all habits is ready' with the total blurred. Unlock = Full Life Audit (unlimited stacked habits, invested-instead curve to 65, Burning Now live widget, quit-streak tracker) at $4.99/week with 3-day trial, or $19.99 lifetime unlock for subscription-averse users (hybrid per 2026 meta).
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 5 days
- **Competitors:** Web-only latte-factor and compound-interest calculators (no product, no share card); Cleo (roasts, but requires bank linking, no lifetime math); Death Clock (same reveal mechanic, different vertical). No polished mobile-native 'lifetime cost of your habit' reveal app owns this.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** The product's own persuasion works against its pricing model — it convinces the buyer that subscriptions like it are the problem.

### 18 Summers — 7.17

Enter your kid's birthday and watch the app burn out the summers you've already used — millennial-parent guilt math turned into a widget, a memory jar, and a poster you'll cry over.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s): Real home video, kid running through a sprinkler, golden hour. Text overlay: "She's 7. I have 11 summers left with her." BEAT 2 (1-4s): Cut to screen recording — parent types name and birthday. Eighteen sun icons appear; seven of them burn out to gray one by one with soft click sounds. BEAT 3 (4-9s): Slow push on the result screen: "11 summers remain" with the sub-stat fading in below: "...and maybe 6 where she still wants to hang out with you." Sparse piano. BEAT 4 (9-13s): Parent visibly puts the phone face-down, scoops up the kid, walks out of frame. Text: "75% of the time you'll ever spend with your kids is used up by age 12." BEAT 5 (13-15s): The share card — an 18-sun grid with the family name and 'Summer 2026' — fills the screen. CTA text: "See how many you have left. Search 18 SUMMERS."
- **Why it spreads:** 'You only get 18 summers' is already one of parenting TikTok's most reposted guilt-stats — this gives it a personalized number and a shareable artifact, which is the exact upgrade path that took 'life in weeks' from talking-head to charting apps. Parents tag their co-parent (built-in 2x reach per view, the Locket/Queue couples mechanic), and every commenter replies with their own remaining count. Launch timing is perfect: it's August — summer just ended, back-to-school content is peaking (ParentSquare is #3 in the US free chart on pure seasonality), and 'that was one of our 18' is the exact sentence circulating right now. Mom-creator seeding is the cheapest aligned niche in the Quittr playbook sense.
- **Target user:** Millennial parents (28-45) of kids under 12, mom-TikTok and dad-guilt audiences; secondary: grandparents counting their own remaining summers with grandkids.
- **Core loop:** Countdown widget ('Summer ends in 12 days' / '287 days until Summer 2027') keeps it on the home screen year-round. During summer: a weekly 'memory jar' prompt — snap one photo, one line, stored locally — so the current summer fills up as the grid burns down. Milestone alerts create re-engagement spikes all year ('last first day of elementary school', 'roughly 300 bedtime stories left'). End of summer: auto-generated 'Summer 2026' recap poster — a second share moment built into the calendar.
- **Monetization:** Free: one child, the burn-down reveal, one share card. Paywall after the reveal: additional kids, the countdown widget, the milestone timeline, unlimited memory-jar photos, and the printable end-of-summer poster. Family category skews annual, so lead with $29.99/year after a 7-day trial, with $4.99/week as the impulse fallback and a $49.99 lifetime family plan.
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 5 days
- **Competitors:** 18 Summers the brand sells physical jars and calendars (validates demand, no app moat); baby-journal subscription apps like Qeepsake own memories but not the countdown gut-punch; life-in-weeks apps are adult-self-focused. The summers-remaining widget plus memory jar combo is unclaimed.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** No curiosity gap: the number is just 18 minus the kid's age, so viewers get the entire emotional payoff from the reel itself and have little reason to install.

### MonkMode — 7.17

A brutally honest 90-second interrogation rates your discipline, brands you with a rank from Dopamine Slave to Monk, and makes you climb the ladder one daily check-in at a time.

- **The reel:** HOOK (spoken + on-screen, 0-2s): 'I asked an app to rate my discipline. It put me in the bottom 6%.' BEAT 1 (2-9s): POV phone screen, rapid-fire quiz questions with the creator answering out loud, wincing: 'First thing you touch in the morning?' → taps 'My phone.' 'Last time you were bored and didn't reach for it?' → taps 'I don't remember.' 'Cold water or warm?' → 'Warm... obviously?' BEAT 2 (9-12s): Loading screen: 'Comparing you against 30-day monks...' BEAT 3 (12-16s): The rank card slams in with a bass hit — black and gold card: 'RANK: DOPAMINE SLAVE. Discipline Score 31/100. Bottom 6%.' BEAT 4 (16-20s): Creator reaction — half-laughing, visibly hurt: 'bro the app is NOT real.' BEAT 5 (20-25s): Swipe to the 7-rank ladder (Slave → Scroller → Novice → Disciple → Ascetic → Stoic → Monk) with 'Estimated time to Monk: 9 months.' BEAT 6 (25-28s): Quick cut: Day 1 check-in completed, streak lit. CTA: 'Search MonkMode. Post your rank in the comments — no lying.' 
- **Why it spreads:** Brutal-honesty ratings farm two comment engines at once: outrage ('this app called me a Dopamine Slave') and humility-bragging ('got Ascetic first try'). The rank is a tribal identity label, which makes the card inherently postable and duet-able — 'what rank did you get' is a self-perpetuating comment format. It's perfectly aligned with the Quittr distribution playbook: seed Christian, fitness, and self-improvement micro-creators at $3/1K views whose audiences already speak 'monk mode' as native slang. Rank-ups give creators serial content ('Week 4: still a Novice'), which converts a one-off viral spike into a recurring series — the retention loop Sora never had.
- **Target user:** 17-26 year-old males in self-improvement / stoicism / gym / NoFap-adjacent TikTok — the demographic Quittr monetized to $250K MRR in 4 months with near-identical creator channels.
- **Core loop:** Daily protocol check-in (phone-free first hour, training, no doomscroll after 10pm, cold shower — user picks 4-6 items) → each completed day earns XP toward the next rank → rank-up triggers a ceremony share card → weekly re-score shows your percentile climbing → miss two days and your rank decays, which is the retention hook; widget shows current rank crest and streak.
- **Monetization:** Free: full quiz + rank reveal + rank share card (the marketing artifact stays free). Hard paywall behind 'Begin Ascension': the full sub-stat report (focus, sleep, diet, dopamine, physique), the personalized 90-day protocol, rank progression, decay protection, widget — $4.99/week with 3-day trial or $39.99/year (this audience proved willing at Quittr's $12.99/mo). Quiz-then-paywall is the top-converting onboarding pattern (10%+ vs 2.7% median).
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 5 days
- **Competitors:** Quittr ($250K MRR, single-vice focus), Rise/Habitica-style trackers (generic, no brutal rating, no rank identity), monk-mode journals and Notion templates. Generic habit trackers are a saturated niche — the wedge is the brutal percentile verdict plus rank identity, which none of them lead with.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** The percentile is invented — there is no comparison population — and this audience loves debunk stitches; one 'I answered randomly and got the same rank' video reframes the whole app as a scam.

### Aura Card — 7.00

A 60-second vibe quiz mints you a holographic, tilt-reactive personal trading card — your aura colors, archetype name, and a rarity percentile your friends will demand to beat.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s, front-camera selfie shot, text overlay): "apparently only 0.8% of people get a gold aura and i'm one of them??" BEAT 2 (1-5s): fast screen-recorded cuts of the three prettiest quiz questions — 'pick the sky that feels like 3am to you', 'which door do you open' — thumb tapping quickly. BEAT 3 (5-10s): the mint animation — a foil pack tears open, the card flips out and the holographic sheen sweeps as the creator physically tilts their phone (gyroscope parallax); card reads aura gradient, archetype "The Midnight Architect", rarity 0.8%. BEAT 4 (10-15s): side-by-side with a friend's card held up on a second phone: "why is yours COMMON," laughing. CTA (text, after 50% watch): "it's called Aura Card — comment your archetype, i'm judging." Comment-farm variant for the series: creator ranks archetypes from the comments in a deliberately controversial tier list.
- **Why it spreads:** Identity flex + rarity scarcity + 'what did you get' comment bait — the same mechanics that made Wrapped-remix apps chart (Verse hit #1 off a Spotify-taste artifact) and Umax spread (a scannable personal verdict). The tilt-reactive foil card is a 3-second visual wow that reads on mute, and rarity percentiles make every comment section a leaderboard. Tier-list ranking videos of archetypes give creators an infinite controversial-series format (Queue's ranking video: 3.3M views).
- **Target user:** Gen Z women and astrology-adjacent 16-28s who already share aura filters, Co-Star screenshots, and 'which character are you' quizzes; K-pop/anime card-collector aesthetic overlap.
- **Core loop:** Daily aura reading: a deterministic daily draw (seeded from birthday + date) shifts your card's energy, border, and one-line reading each morning — streak counter for consecutive daily draws, with seasonal frames as streak rewards. Monthly 'Aura Wrapped' recaps your month of draws as a new card. Widget shows today's aura color so the ritual lives on the home screen.
- **Monetization:** Free: the quiz, your base card, archetype, and rarity — one share allowed. Hard-ish paywall at the reveal for the full 15-page aura report ('your shadow trait', 'who drains you'), gold/holo foil themes, unlimited friend-compatibility readings, and the daily-reading widget. $3.99/week with 3-day trial or $24.99/year — Umax-style curiosity-gap pricing.
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 7 days
- **Competitors:** Co-Star and The Pattern (astrology reports, no collectible artifact), TikTok aura filters (no depth, no app), random aura quiz websites (ugly, no card). The collectible trading-card presentation of self is unowned.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** The signature wow (tilt foil) only exists in video; the shared static card loses the effect, weakening the screenshot-and-share leg the loop depends on.

### Scrolled Away — 6.83

Type your age and daily screen time; a life-in-weeks grid floods with black ink showing the years of your remaining life you're about to spend on your phone — then a slider shows you buying them back.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s): Creator holds their phone showing the real iOS Screen Time page ('TikTok — 3h 41m'). Text overlay: "You'll spend 11 years of what's left of your life on this app." BEAT 2 (1-4s): Screen recording — they type age (24) and daily screen time (6h 12m) into the app. BEAT 3 (4-9s): A life-in-weeks grid draws itself: gray squares for weeks already lived — then black ink bleeds through the remaining weeks like a stain, swallowing 11 years' worth. Counter ticks up: "4,160 days. 11.4 years. Scrolled." The only sound is a phone's silent-mode buzz. BEAT 4 (9-12s): Second punch, text only over the stained grid: "That's more time than you'll spend with your parents, ever again — combined." BEAT 5 (12-15s): They drag a slider to '90 min/day' and the ink visibly retreats: "+7.2 years back." CTA: "Free to see your number. Search SCROLLED AWAY." Viewer realizes they're watching this mid-doomscroll — that irony is the punchline.
- **Why it spreads:** The format attacks the viewer in the exact moment of the sin — you see it while doomscrolling, which makes it a pattern-interrupt with built-in self-recognition ('posting this from hour 4' comments write themselves). It's the Death Clock emotional-calculator genre pointed at Gen Z's most self-aware anxiety, and the poisoned-grid card is a stark, instantly legible screenshot artifact. Massive aligned creator supply: dopamine-detox, self-improvement, and looksmaxxing-adjacent accounts (the Quittr/Umax seeding lanes) monetize this worldview already and will take $50-100 posts. Slight rage-bait edge ('delete this app after watching') farms shares without Cluely-level brand damage.
- **Target user:** Self-aware doomscrollers 16-28 in the dopamine-detox / self-improvement TikTok orbit; secondary: parents who run the numbers on their teens.
- **Core loop:** Weekly check-in: enter your real screen-time number (30 seconds, from iOS Settings) and watch the ink advance or retreat on your grid — your life visibly gets longer when you scroll less. 'Years reclaimed' counter and streak; home-screen widget shows your stained grid so the cost of opening TikTok is on the same screen as TikTok. Each weekly delta generates a fresh before/after share card.
- **Monetization:** Free: the full first reveal and one share card (the reveal is the marketing). Hard-ish paywall immediately after: the reclaim slider and plan, weekly recalibration with streaks, the widget, and the comparison stat pack ('vs. time with parents', 'vs. every vacation you'll ever take'). Pure utilities meta: $5.99/week with a 3-day trial, $29.99/year anchor. Day-0 conversion focus since viral traffic monetizes in the first session or never.
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 3 days
- **Competitors:** Opal, one sec, and Jomo are VC-backed blockers (~$99/yr) that sell intervention, not the mortality-math reveal; StayFree shows lifetime projections buried in stats screens; scattered web calculators have no artifact, widget, or loop. The grief-grid as the product is open.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** The paid tier is data entry plus arithmetic; users who feel the guilt strongly enough to pay will pay a blocker instead, and everyone else got the full experience free.

### Last Times — 6.83

The friendship mortality calculator: it counts how many more times you'll actually see each person you love, and shows the remaining hangouts as a dot grid you can't unsee.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s, face-to-camera, on-screen text): 'I just found out I'll only see my best friend 23 more times. Ever.' BEATS: (1s-5s) screen-record: types 'Emma', taps 'we see each other about 2x a year' (she moved cities), both ages. (5s-9s) the grid renders: a wall of grey dots for hangouts already spent, then a tiny cluster of 23 glowing dots left — camera pushes in on how small it is. (9s-14s) creator goes quiet, adds 'Mom, 4x a year' -> 18 dots. Adds 'college group chat, 1x a year' -> 9 dots. (14s-18s) overlay text: 'we think we have forever. we have dots.' (18s-22s) CTA: 'the app is called Last Times — run it for your best friend and send them their number. I'm booking a flight.' Comments section becomes the content: people posting their numbers and tagging the friend.
- **Why it spreads:** Direct heir to the two most proven emotional-stat formats on the platform — 'how many times will you see your parents again' (~4.9M likes) and life-in-weeks grids — but aimed at friendships, which nobody has productized, and engineered as a send-THIS-to-them loop: the natural CTA of every video is DMing your friend their own number. Grief plus guilt plus a hauntingly small integer equals maximum comment velocity, and the dot-grid card is a one-glance screenshot that explains the whole app.
- **Target user:** 22-35 year olds in the post-college friendship-drift years (moved cities, group chat went quiet) — the demo already crying to 'we don't hang out anymore' audio. Secondary: anyone with aging parents, the proven adjacent market.
- **Core loop:** Add a person -> answer 3 inputs (how often you see them, ages, drift trajectory) -> dot-grid reveal + 'see them 1 more time per year = +14 dots' what-if slider -> share the card / send them their number -> add your whole roster to build your Friendship Portfolio -> home-screen widget shows the dots and a 'next hangout' countdown, and logging a hangout marks a dot gold — the retention ritual.
- **Monetization:** Free: one person's grid and one watermarked share card. Paywall on adding a second person ('your whole roster deserves a number'): $2.99/week with 3-day trial, $19.99/year, or $14.99 lifetime. Paid unlocks unlimited people, the Friendship Portfolio view, what-if sliders, hangout logging, and the widget. Cheap impulse pricing because the trigger is an emotional spike from a reel.
- **Client-side v1:** yes · est. 4 days
- **Competitors:** Life-in-weeks apps (Lifev, A Life In Weeks) and the seeyourfolks.com parents calculator prove the mechanic but stop at mortality-of-self or parents; no iOS app owns friendship countdowns, and none have a per-person portfolio, what-if slider, or widget. Low technical moat, so speed to the reel format and card polish are the defense.
- **Judge-flagged flaws:** The paid unit (additional people) is a repetition of transparent math, sold on the panel's weakest emotional trigger.

### Saturdays — 6.83

Type your birthday and see exactly how many Saturdays you have left — then how many you have left with the people you love — rendered as a haunting, beautiful countdown card.

- **The reel:** HOOK (0-1s, on-screen text over a finger hovering on a date picker, sound-off readable): "how many Saturdays do you actually have left?" BEAT 2 (1-4s): screen recording — creator types their birthday, taps COUNT; a full-screen grid of 4,680 dots ignites and the lived ones burn out in fast-forward, slamming to a stop on a glowing counter: "2,143 Saturdays left." BEAT 3 (4-8s): creator adds "my mom, 58" — a second, much smaller number materializes under it: "Saturdays left with mom: 611." Hard cut to the creator's face going quiet. BEAT 4 (8-13s): the polished share card — black card, gold grid, two numbers, their first name — gets saved to Photos and posted to their story. CTA (~60% mark, spoken + text): "don't do this one unless you're ready — it's called Saturdays, just search it on the App Store." Reaction/duet format is built in: every stitch is someone else's number.
- **Why it spreads:** It is the proven emotional-stat reveal family (Jesse Itzler's see-your-parents math pulled ~4.9M likes; 'your life in 4,500 weeks' grids are a recurring format) compressed into one personal, comparison-inducing number. Comments grieve and share their own counts, stitches write themselves ('I did the Saturdays thing and I'm not okay'), and the card IS the ad — every story post shows the app's output with zero explanation needed. Press writes this hook for free (Death Clock's earned-media loop) without the morbid death-date liability.
- **Target user:** 18-34 TikTok/IG users in a sentimental scroll — especially people who live away from their parents; secondary: productivity/memento-mori audience that already shares 4,000-weeks content.
